## Design Context

### Users
Mid-30s professionals (often WFH) who've outsourced cooking to meal kits and delivery apps. They think they have a time problem but actually have a skill problem. They're smart, curious, a little burned out on the conveyor belt of convenience food. They land on the site from LinkedIn content, flyers, or word of mouth. Context: browsing on laptop or phone, probably during a work break or evening scroll.

### Brand Personality
**Three words:** Intuitive, handmade, magnetic

Food Jazz is a 1-on-1 culinary coaching program that teaches kitchen improvisation — ratios, flavor vocabulary, pattern recognition — not recipes. The brand should feel like a refreshingly new way to think about cooking. Not a cooking school. Not a meal kit. Something that didn't exist before.

**Emotional goals (in order):**
1. Novelty — "I've never seen anything like this"
2. Seen — "This person understands my exact situation"
3. Excitement — "I want to do this"
4. Empowerment — "I could actually pull this off"
5. Relatability — Akshay feels like someone you'd want to hang out with, not a chef on a pedestal

**Voice:** Direct, casual, warm. One glass of wine, not three. No AI-isms, no rhetorical flourishes. Say the thing. Read the Voice Bank before writing any copy.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Modern, hip, a little abstract. Closer to a thoughtful tech brand or indie creative studio than a cooking website. Handmade touches (Caveat headings, hand-drawn spiral, decorative vegetables) keep it from feeling cold, but the underlying structure and spacing should feel considered and contemporary.

**Theme:** Light mode (cream base). Dark mode planned for later — CSS tokens are already set up for the switch. One charcoal section per page max.

**What it IS:**
- Organic but refined — sage greens, warm creams, generous whitespace
- A little magical, but understated — sparkle motifs, subtle glow effects, not fantasy
- Improvisational and intuitive in spirit (never literal jazz references)
- Typography-forward — Caveat display + DM Sans body, clear hierarchy, readable at all sizes
- Breathing room everywhere — sections at 9rem+ padding, generous gaps

**What it is NOT:**
- Classic cooking website aesthetic (cluttered, recipe-card grids, food photography overload)
- Lo-fi or rough HTML — this should feel polished and intentional
- Jazz-literal (no music metaphors, instruments, scales, "playing in key")
- Corporate or sterile
- Dark/techy/neon — no cyan-on-dark, no purple gradients
- Emoji-heavy or performative

**Anti-references:** Standard food blogs, recipe aggregators, meal kit landing pages (HelloFresh, Blue Apron). Anything that looks like a WordPress theme for food content.

**Color palette (established):**
- `--sage: #7A8C6E` (primary)
- `--cream: #FAF7F2` (background)
- `--charcoal: #2E2E2C` (text, dark sections)
- `--golden: #D4A843` (sparkle accents only)
- `--warm-gray: #5C5A56` (body text)
- Sections alternate cream / `--sage-wash: #EDF2EB`
- Extended: `--linen`, `--wood`, `--terracotta` for supporting warmth

**Typography (established):**
- Display: Caveat (600-700 weight), fluid sizing via clamp()
- Body: DM Sans (weight 300), base 20px (1rem)
- Labels/eyebrows: 0.6rem, uppercase, wide tracking
- Never drop below 12px rendered

**Spacing (established):**
- 4pt scale: `--space-xs` through `--space-xl`
- Major sections: minimum 9rem padding
- Container max: 1100px, reading column: 680px
- Horizontal padding: 2rem desktop, 1.25rem tablet, 1rem phone

### Design Principles

1. **Breathing room is the brand.** Generous whitespace isn't wasted space — it's what makes Food Jazz feel different from every cluttered cooking site. When in doubt, add more space, not more content.

2. **Handmade warmth, modern bones.** The structure should feel contemporary and considered (clean grids, fluid typography, smooth transitions). The personality comes through in texture — Caveat headings, hand-drawn motifs, subtle organic touches layered on top.

3. **One idea per section.** Each scroll-stop should land a single clear thought. No compound sections trying to do three things. Let the message breathe.

4. **Restraint is the flex.** One SVG motif per section, not combinations. One charcoal section per page. One accent color moment. The design earns trust through what it chooses NOT to do.

5. **Mobile is the real product.** Most visitors arrive from LinkedIn on their phone. Every section must work at 390px before it works at 1440px. Touch targets, readable type, no hidden functionality.

### Accessibility Notes
- `prefers-reduced-motion` support already implemented — maintain it
- Base font size 20px with weight 300 keeps body text readable for older audiences
- Dark mode planned (CSS custom properties already structured for it)
- No specific WCAG target, but maintain good contrast ratios and readable type sizes
- Minimum rendered size: 12px for any text element
